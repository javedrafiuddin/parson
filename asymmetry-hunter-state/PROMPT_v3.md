## SECTION 5 — THE SCREEN (staged funnel; a name that fails a stage is out, reason logged; no favorites carried through)

### 5.1 Definitions — use these formulas exactly, everywhere
- Drawdown: current price vs. the split-adjusted 3-year high.
- Enterprise value: market cap on FULLY DILUTED shares (Section 5.3) + total debt + preferred liquidation value − cash.
- Cash runway: (cash − debt and preferred obligations maturing within 12 months) ÷ trailing-4-quarter operating cash burn, expressed in YEARS (and months in parentheses). If operating cash flow is positive, runway = "cash-positive" and the test is passed.
- Spread %: (ask − bid) ÷ mid.
- Wave window: the multi-year period over which a theme wave could plausibly form and crest for this name — default 5 years, argued per name in Section 5.6, never shorter than 2 years.
- Durability horizon: how long the chosen instrument can legally and mechanically keep the holder exposed — to expiry, redemption, call, or maturity — compared against the wave window.

- Priced for failure (at least one; two or more is the archetype): drawdown ≥ 60% from the 3-year high; OR enterprise value < 2.0x trailing revenue; OR market cap < $500M with trailing revenue > $20M.

- SURVIVAL — scored, not just gated (this is a first-class factor in this edition, because the thesis needs the company alive in year 3, not just quarter 3): minimum gate = runway ≥ 12 months by the 5.1 formula, or a filed, CLOSED financing that extends it (an announced intention does not). - Reverse-split risk: flag any name under $1.00 with an exchange deficiency notice or a split proposal in a proxy; must be named as a risk in output.

- Fraud red flags — ANY ONE is an automatic exclusion until resolved in a filing: auditor resignation/dismissal with disagreement; going-concern opinion combined with delinquent filings; SEC trading suspension; restated financials within 12 months without a clean subsequent audit; CEO/CFO departure disclosed alongside an internal investigation.

## SECTION 6 — THE MATH (multi-year; every number shown with its arithmetic; no candidate advances on narrative)

### 6.1 The Fermi decomposition — re-aimed at years, two numbers, never one
For every candidate compute the UNFILTERED base product first:
P(survival) × P(theme wave) × P(company leads) × P(holder captures) = p4_base.
The factors are re-defined for the white-swan horizon — operational definitions a second analyst could reproduce:
- P(survival): the fraction of a defined historical cohort of similar names (state the cohort: e.g., US-listed names with this drawdown/runway profile) still listed, tradable, and not diluted into irrelevance FIVE years later. Survival here means surviving the whole wave window — a company that dies in year 2 scores zero no matter how good years 0–1 looked.
- P(theme wave): the fraction of comparable historical theme episodes (state them) in which a genuine wave formed over a multi-year window — operationally, the episode's leader basket rising ≥ 10x trough-to-peak within 5 years. This replaces any near-term catalyst probability; there is no credit for a wave "starting soon," only for waves of this type having formed at all from conditions like today's.
- P(company leads): conditional on the wave forming, the fraction of cohort names that captured at least half the leader basket's move — leadership, not participation.
- P(holder captures): the fraction of holders of THIS instrument who could actually realize the move across the whole window — given the Section 5.4 durability analysis (expiry vs. wave window, redemption/call caps, roll feasibility), the dilution trajectory of Section 5.3 eroding the per-share claim, and exit liquidity at the far end. A correct thesis in an instrument that expires, gets redeemed away, or is diluted to a stub scores zero here.
Calibration (start here, argue only with evidence): 0.50 × 0.125 × 0.225 × 0.15 = 0.00211 ≈ 1-in-474, call it 1-in-500 — the estimated rate for a random "graveyard" convex instrument reaching a QBTS-class outcome. On a multi-year horizon the unfiltered truth is likely worse, and your cohort work in Section 8 exists to replace these placeholders with measured rates; where the cohort gives a measured base rate, the measured rate governs and the placeholder is retired with a note.
Then, separately, your FILTERED estimate: p4_filtered = p4_base × LIFT, where LIFT is the product of per-factor lifts your screen claims. The lift is a separate, challengable line item: for each factor state (a) the calibration value, (b) your filtered value, (c) the evidence, (d) what evidence would move it down. Example of the required honesty: "Survival 0.50 → 0.80 on 3.1 years of runway and a closed financing = 1.6x lift; joint 0.00338 = 1-in-296."
Dependence caveat (print it): these factors are NOT independent — a company that leads a forming wave is likelier to survive, raise cheap capital, and be capturable — so the product is a rough skeleton, not a measurement. Report the product as a RANGE: recompute with each factor at its defensible low and high. A point estimate presented as precise is a failure of this section.

State EV per $1 and on the position's proposed allocation from Section 7.3. Also report the same EV with the White Swan row capped at 50x and at 25x, so the verdict does not secretly depend on the 190x tail.
Breakeven, stated correctly: with payoff M (net), breakeven p = 1 ÷ M on the White Swan term alone — at 190x gross that is 1-in-190 BEFORE costs and dilution; at a cost-and-dilution-adjusted net figure it is materially worse, and the muddle/re-rate rows shift it further. For LEAPS under a roll plan: rolls happen per the 5.4 plan — at least 90 days before expiry, at the monthly review nearest the roll trigger, with the roll cost re-checked against the 7.2 table before any roll ticket is proposed. A missed roll window converts the position to the exit path below; it is never rolled in a panic inside 30 days of expiry.
- EXIT (any one suffices): the dated invalidation (6.4) occurs; a 7.4 standing trigger fires (fraud, delisting, instrument event with no viable exercise/roll path, wave case falsified by primary evidence); OR the thesis WINS — the wave arrives, the name re-rates to the Re-rate/White Swan path of its own Section 6.2 scenario table, and the pre-written trim plan executes: state the trim plan per position at entry (default: sell into strength in tranches at the 10x, 25x, and 50x net multiples, sized in the doctrine — e.g., recover the full set's $800 by the first tranche where possible — with the final tranche a deliberate hold-or-fold decision at the 5-year path review). "It went up a lot" without the scenario path being reached is not an exit; reaching the path and refusing to trim is also a doctrine violation — both are named here so future sessions can enforce them.
- ACQUISITION: a cash takeout ends the position at the deal price (log it, done); a stock takeout converts the thesis — the default is exit at the first monthly review after close unless a fresh analysis under a new instruction adopts the acquirer's thesis. A capped acquisition far below the white-swan path is the bear case arriving by another door; the doctrine is to take it, not to wait for a better bid that the filings do not promise.

## SECTION 8 — THE ML MECHANISM (build it to working code; its job all week is ranking the field by white-swan probability)

This system nests under the user's existing study of Jansen's "Machine Learning for Trading." Point-in-time discipline is non-negotiable. The model learns from the Section 5 rubric and Section 6 decomposition; it does not hunt price patterns alone. In v3 this section is BUILT, not just designed: schemas, feature builder, cohort constructor, label generator, and validation harness exist as code in the project folder by the end of Run Stage R4. The model's single job for the week is to rank the current candidate field by long-term white-swan probability; the human rubric ranks independently, and Section 8.7 forces the two to confront each other.

### 8.1 Data layer (build this first)
A survivorship-bias-free universe: every US-listed common stock, warrant, unit, and OTC name that traded at any point in the study window — including delisted, bankrupt, acquired, reverse-split, and halted names. Never drop a ticker because it later died: for this system the dead names are half the training signal. Four point-in-time tables, columnar Parquet partitioned by as_of_date and ticker, queried via DuckDB:
1. PRICES: daily OHLCV, bid/ask where available, volume, halt flags, split/dividend-adjusted and unadjusted prices, source_timestamp.
2. FILINGS: CIK, form type, period end, and FILING TIMESTAMP (EDGAR accepted datetime — the tradable date), with extracted cash, debt, shares outstanding, warrants outstanding, ATM/shelf capacity, going-concern language. Store values AS ORIGINALLY FILED; amendments and restatements are new versioned rows — never overwrite.
3. CATALYST & WAVE CALENDAR: ticker, type (earnings, PDUFA, trial readout, contract award, index inclusion, lockup expiry, investor day — plus theme-wave markers: sector basket composition changes, anchor-program awards, standard adoptions), scheduled date, DATE FIRST PUBLICLY KNOWN, source, and actual outcome date. Features may only use an event once its date_first_public ≤ decision time — facts known only in hindsight are leakage.
4. INSTRUMENT TERMS: underlying, strike, expiry, exercise ratio, redemption/call terms, anti-dilution clauses, cashless-exercise provisions, last amendment date, and instrument end date (expiry, redemption, call, maturity, delisting) — the durability facts of Section 5.4 in machine-readable form.
Deliverables: a data dictionary and schema for all four tables (primary keys, timestamp definitions), plus one worked example of a filing becoming usable only at max(filing_timestamp, next market open) — the period-end date is never the usable date.

### 8.2 The cohorts — white swans and the graveyard (the heart of this edition's ML)
Construct BOTH cohorts from the survivorship-free universe, on point-in-time entry conditions — this construction is documented in `cohort_report.md` with counts at every step:
- ENTRY CONDITION (applied identically to both cohorts, historically): a name-date qualifies as a "buyable base" if, using only data available on that date, the name met the priced-for-failure criteria of Section 5.2 (drawdown, EV/revenue, or market-cap/revenue test) AND the tradability floors of Section 4 (listed or SEC-reporting OTC, minimum dollar volume). Bases are sampled at a fixed cadence (e.g., quarter-ends) so the cohort is a defined population, not a cherry-picked set of famous starting lines.
- WHITE-SWAN COHORT (the positive class): every buyable-base name-date whose security subsequently achieved, within a forward window of ≤ 5 years, EITHER a peak multiple ≥ 25x from the base-date ask (split-adjusted, measured on prices a holder could actually transact at — daily closes, never intraday spikes on no volume) OR a terminal (5-year or instrument-end) net realizable multiple ≥ 10x after the Section 7.2 costs. Record both multiples per member. QBTS's own base dates are members if they qualify — one member among many, with no special weight.
- GRAVEYARD COHORT (the negative class): every buyable-base name-date whose security, within the same forward window, expired worthless, was delisted to no bid, fell > 95% without recovery, or was diluted into a stub (fully diluted value at window end < 10% of base-date value). Everything neither swan nor graveyard is the MUDBLE class — keep it as a third class for calibration, never silently merge it into either pole.
- THE IMBALANCE, STATED AND HANDLED: white swans will be a small fraction of buyable bases — plausibly well under 1%. This is the defining statistical fact of the problem, and the build must confront it in writing: (i) report the raw counts and the true base rate before any modeling; (ii) case-control sampling or class weights MAY be used for fitting, but every probability the system outputs must be corrected back to the TRUE population prevalence — a probability quoted off a resampled distribution is a fabrication and is forbidden; (iii) model quality is judged on precision-at-the-top (of the names ranked highest, what fraction were swans or strong muddles?) and on Brier/log-loss against the climatology forecast (the raw base rate), never on accuracy — a model that predicts "never a swan" for everything is ~99% accurate and worthless, and the validation must say so if that's what the numbers show.
- SURVIVORSHIP TRAPS IN THIS TRAINING SET (each one named, each one tested for): training only on names still listed today; identifying historical names by their CURRENT ticker/name/CIK mappings (dead names get recycled tickers — match on CIK and date, never ticker alone); features computed from restated financials instead of as-filed values; peak multiples computed on split-adjusted series without checking the name was actually tradable at the base date (halts, expert-market periods, no-bid stretches break the fantasy entry); warrant cohorts built only from warrants whose agreements happen to survive in EDGAR exhibits (state the coverage rate of the terms table and treat uncovered warrant bases as their own labeled subset, not as silently absent).

### 8.3 Labels — long-horizon outcomes, honestly measured
For each buyable-base date, entering at the next session's ask: (a) MAX FAVORABLE EXCURSION multiples at 1, 3, and 5 years (peak close-based multiple within each window) — used ONLY to classify swan-hood for the cohort and as a diagnostic, never quoted as an achievable return, because exit at a peak close is not an executable plan, especially in illiquid warrants; (b) TERMINAL net realizable multiples at 1, 3, and 5 years and at instrument end — exit at the BID under the Section 7.2 cost table, including dilution-adjusted per-share values for common stock — this is the payoff label the EV wiring in 8.6 consumes; (c) terminal failure label: went_to_zero_or_delisted = 1 if the vehicle expires worthless, is delisted to no bid, or falls > 95% without recovery inside the window. A horizon with no available quote is UNRESOLVED — never dropped, never filled with a stale price; unresolved windows shorten — they never silently become zeros or wins. Deliverable: label-generation pseudocode that provably touches no post-decision filing revisions.

### 8.4 Features (every one computable from data with timestamp ≤ decision time)
Priced-for-failure (Section 5.1 formulas: drawdown, EV/revenue, market cap/cash, short interest ÷ float); runway and balance sheet (cash, burn over last 2 filed quarters, runway years per 5.1, debt due < 12 months, current ratio); dilution (shelf/ATM remaining, (warrants + converts) ÷ shares outstanding, S-1/S-3 filed within 90 days, reverse split within prior 12 months, authorized-share headroom, historical issuance rate); durability (instrument years-to-expiry/call/maturity vs. the 5-year label window, redemption trigger distance from current price); wave context (a defined peer basket's trailing returns and volume trend, sector-level contract/adoption counts from primary sources, count of peers up > 20% in trailing windows — all lagged to decision time; NO feature may encode "a wave later happened"); liquidity/float (free float, 20-day ADV, median spread %, position size as % of ADV). Deliverable: a feature schema listing each feature, its formula, source table, and lag rule.

### 8.5 Leakage controls (enforced in code, with tests) + validation + sensitivity sweeps
Purged walk-forward splits; purge any training sample whose label window overlaps the test window, plus an embargo of at least the MAXIMUM LABEL HORIZON OF THE LABEL SET IN USE after each test block — for the 5-year labels that is a 5-year embargo; a shorter embargo against 5-year labels is leakage, not caution. (Where the study window cannot support the full embargo for the 5-year set, use the longest feasible embargo, state the compromise in `cohort_report.md`, and mark the affected validation as PARTIALLY_PURGED — never present it as clean.) Filing-lag rule per 8.1. Corporate actions applied using ex-dates known at the time; reverse splits and ticker/CIK changes flagged so dead names are never silently merged or dropped. Clustering: all overlapping windows for the same ticker form one cluster kept whole in train or test — AND theme is a clustering unit too, AND calendar era is a clustering unit for wave labels (ten quantum names in one sector melt-up, or fifty names in one market-wide mania, are one wave observation, not fifty).
VALIDATION: expanding-window walk-forward on the cohort: train on matured labels to year Y, test the next 12 months of base dates, roll. All results NET of the Section 7.2 cost table. Benchmarks on the identical filtered universe and dates: (a) the human rubric alone (Sections 5–6 scoring, as code), ranked by rubric score; (b) random selection from the same universe, 1,000 bootstrap draws. The model must beat BOTH on precision-at-the-top and on Brier/log-loss vs. climatology. It does not win on raw return or accuracy.
SENSITIVITY SWEEPS (mandatory, per Section 3.3): re-run the final ranking under perturbed assumptions — swan threshold at 20x and 35x alongside 25x; forward window at 4 and 6 years alongside 5; cost assumptions at 0.5x and 2x the 7.2 table; cohort entry cadence shifted one quarter. A current candidate's deliverable rank is the RANGE of its ranks across the sweep, with the median configuration named. Rank instability is itself a reported feature: a name whose rank collapses under small perturbations is flagged FRAGILE in the ranked table.

### 8.6 The model — and its pre-flight reality check
MANDATORY PRE-FLIGHT: before proposing any model, count the cohort label events: how many white-swan (25x-peak) outcomes, how many 10x-terminal outcomes, how many graveyard outcomes exist in the dataset. Print the counts. If white-swan positives number fewer than 30, there is NO trainable swan class: the ML deliverable degrades to the human rubric plus base-rate tables plus the muddle/graveyard model if ITS counts permit, gradient boosting on the swan class is FORBIDDEN, and you say why in one paragraph. If the count permits a model: elastic-net logistic regression for P(swan by year 5) and P(zero/delisted) is the champion; a shallow, heavily regularized gradient-boosted ranker (max depth 2–3, strong shrinkage, minimum child weight enforced) is challenger only, promoted only if it beats logistic on purged out-of-sample folds on the 8.5 criteria. No deep learning. Calibrate (isotonic or Platt, on purged folds) with the true-prevalence correction of 8.2 applied, and wire predictions into Section 6: EV = P(wave-and-lead outcome) × net payoff multiple − P(failure) × loss − costs, with the model's P(swan) shown next to — never substituted for — the human Fermi p4_filtered. Report calibration as predicted-vs-realized by decile AND Brier/log-loss against the base-rate forecast. Retraining after this run happens only under new instructions and only when at least 20 new long-horizon labels have matured — which, given the label horizons, means years; the model frozen at R4 is the model the buy set used, and its version is logged on every ledger entry.

### 8.7 Human–model cross-check
Score every candidate twice, blind: the human rubric and Section 6 Fermi are completed before the model rank is seen; the model never receives the human score as a feature. Disagreement — model top-quartile vs. human bottom-half (or the reverse), opposite EV signs, or P(zero) differing by > 25 points — triggers a written reconciliation note and EXCLUSION from the buy set (the name may remain a finalist in the shadow book for future learning, but the $800 does not follow an unresolved disagreement — with one deployment and no re-runs, there is no shadow-only live tier). A name flagged FRAGILE in the 8.5 sweep carries the flag into the cross-check and must be argued explicitly if it is to reach the buy set. Vetoes: the USER can veto any buy-set name at any time before actuation for any reason and cannot be overridden — a veto is an interruption he chooses, not a checkpoint you wait for, and its absence is never a blocker (a vetoed name's allocation stays in cash unless he directs otherwise in the same message). The MODEL can veto only on data-integrity or tradability grounds (leakage flag, stale filing, no quote, spread beyond the 7.2 table) and cannot be overridden by anyone.
